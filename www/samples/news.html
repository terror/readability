Riverside tram line approved after marathon council session

By Mira Castellanos

Share Post Email

After nearly eleven hours of public comment, the city council voted seven to two early on Saturday morning to approve the long-debated riverside tram line, a twelve-stop route that will connect the old docks to the university campus by 2029.

The decision ends a planning process that began more than a decade ago, when a group of residents first proposed reusing the abandoned freight rail corridor along the river. Since then, the project has survived three feasibility studies, two changes of administration and a lengthy dispute over where the maintenance depot should go.

What the plan includes

The approved design calls for a mostly level route with dedicated lanes for most of its length. Trams will run every six minutes during peak hours and every twelve minutes in the evening.

  • Twelve stations, four of them with covered bicycle parking
  • A new pedestrian bridge at the foot of Lantern Street
  • A maintenance depot on the former cannery site
  • Signal priority at nineteen intersections

Engineers estimate that the full trip from the docks to the campus will take twenty-two minutes, roughly half the time of the current bus route during rush hour.

This corridor has been sitting empty for thirty years. Tonight we finally decided what it is for.

Not everyone was pleased. Several shop owners along Ferry Road argued that two years of construction would drive away customers, and the council agreed to set aside a relief fund for affected businesses. Others questioned whether ridership projections were realistic given how many students already cycle.

Cost and timeline

PhaseWorkExpected completion
1Utility relocation and depot2027
2Track, stations and bridge2028
3Testing and opening2029

The project is expected to cost just under four hundred million, with roughly two thirds coming from regional transport grants. The remainder will be covered by a combination of municipal bonds and a modest increase in parking fees downtown.

Construction on the first phase could begin as soon as next spring, pending a final environmental review of the riverbank near the cannery site.
